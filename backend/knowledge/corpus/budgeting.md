# Budgeting basics

A budget is a plan for money before you spend it. For most people, a simple
category budget is usually enough: a monthly limit for food, one for transport,
one for entertainment, and so on. The point is not to predict perfectly, but to
notice early when a category is running hot.

## A simple framework

One well-known split is roughly half of income to needs, thirty percent to
wants, and twenty percent to saving or paying down debt. Treat the exact
percentages as a starting point, not a rule. If your rent is high, needs will be
larger and something else has to give.

## Reading a budget

Compare spending so far this month against the limit and against how much of the
month has passed. Being at eighty percent of a category with a week left is a
warning; being over the limit is a breach. A breach is not a moral failure — it
is a signal to slow that category down or move money from a softer one.

## Budgets and single purchases

A budget tells you whether a category has room. It does not tell you whether a
purchase is safe for your overall cash flow — that depends on your balance,
buffer and upcoming bills. Use both: category room from the budget, and future
impact from a projection.
