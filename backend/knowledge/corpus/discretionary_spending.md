# Discretionary spending

Discretionary spending is everything you choose to buy that is not an essential
or a fixed commitment. Eating out, gadgets, clothes, entertainment, hobbies and
gifts are discretionary. Rent, groceries, transport to class and existing
subscriptions are not.

## Why the distinction is useful

Essentials and commitments are mostly fixed in the short term, so the room you
have to adjust your finances is almost entirely in the discretionary category.
When money is tight, this is the lever you can actually pull this week.

## The discretionary buffer

Your discretionary buffer is what is left after you subtract your safety buffer
and your known upcoming commitments from your current balance. It is a rough
answer to "how much can I spend freely right now without planning to?". A
positive discretionary buffer means a purchase of that size is comfortable; a
small or negative one means a purchase would come out of money you have already
mentally spent.

## Practical habit

Give large discretionary purchases a short cooling-off period. Checking the
future impact first — projected minimum balance, safety buffer, upcoming bills —
turns an impulse into a decision.
