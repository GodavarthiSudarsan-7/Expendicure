# Emergency fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for genuine emergencies: a medical bill, an
urgent trip home, a broken laptop you need for coursework, a sudden loss of
income. It is separate from your safety buffer. The safety buffer smooths normal
monthly noise; the emergency fund covers rare, larger events.

## How much

For a student, a first target of three to four weeks of essential spending is
reasonable. Once that is in place, growing it toward two to three months gives
real resilience. Build it gradually — a small, automatic transfer each time
income lands is more reliable than occasional large ones.

## Where to keep it

Keep it accessible but slightly out of the way: a separate savings account you
do not carry a card for. The friction is the point — it should be easy to reach
in a real emergency and slightly annoying to raid for a sale.

## Emergency fund versus a purchase

If buying something now would mean dipping into money you are holding for
emergencies, that is a strong signal to wait. Protecting the fund is almost
always worth more than getting the item a few weeks sooner.
