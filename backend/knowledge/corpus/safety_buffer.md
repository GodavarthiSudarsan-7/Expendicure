# Safety buffer

A safety buffer is a minimum balance you decide never to spend below. It is a
line in the sand, not a savings goal. Its job is to absorb the surprises that
every month contains — a bill that is larger than expected, income that lands a
few days late, a fee you forgot about — without pushing you into an overdraft or
forcing you to borrow.

## Why it matters

When your projected balance dips below your safety buffer, you are one small
shock away from real trouble. Keeping the buffer intact means a bad week stays a
bad week instead of becoming a bad month.

## How large should it be

A common starting point for a student is two to four weeks of essential
spending: rent share, food, transport, and any fixed subscriptions. If your
income is irregular, lean toward the higher end.

## Using it with a purchase decision

Before a discretionary purchase, look at the projected minimum balance for the
weeks ahead, not just today's balance. If the purchase would take that projected
minimum below your buffer, the safer choice is usually to wait until a known
inflow lands, or to spend less. The buffer is only useful if you actually
protect it.
